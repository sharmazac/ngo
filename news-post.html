We asked partners what we get wrong

Every two years an outside researcher interviews our partners without us in the room. This is what came back.

Published
26 August 2026
Written by
T. Okafor, Director of Programs
Reading time
9 minutes
Filed under
Transparency

We pay a researcher we do not employ to interview our partners without us in the room, and we publish whatever comes back. This is the fourth round. It is the first where the main complaint was not about money.

What they said

Sixty-one of the 126 partners took part. The interviews are semi-structured and anonymised before we see them, which is the only reason they are worth reading — people will tell a stranger things they will not put in a grant report.

The headline finding was speed. Approvals that we think of as taking "a couple of weeks" were measured at nineteen working days on average, and the tail is worse: one in six waited more than a month. For an organisation running on a term timetable, a month is a term.

You are the most patient funder we have and the slowest. Both of those come from the same place, and only one of them helps us.

The three things we are changing

  • Approvals under $5,000 no longer go to the full panel. The program lead signs, and the panel reviews after the fact.
  • We publish a decision date when we acknowledge a request, and we are measured against it in next year's survey.
  • Payments move to a fixed fortnightly run, so nobody is waiting on a person being back at their desk.

The one we are not acting on

Several partners asked us to fund core costs indefinitely rather than on the five-year cycle. We understand the reasoning — the cycle creates a cliff, and the cliff is stressful. We are keeping it anyway.

The exit date is what makes the rest of the model work. It is why partners build their own funding relationships in years three and four, and it is why nine of the eleven programs we have closed are still running. Removing it would make us more comfortable and them more dependent.

We would rather argue about this in public than quietly drop it, so it is in the annual report too, alongside the number of partners who raised it.

Read the full survey

The researcher's report is published unedited, including the sections that are critical of the survey design itself. It sits with the annual accounts and filings.
